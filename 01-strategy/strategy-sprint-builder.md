# Strategy Sprint Builder — Fable Growth

## Scenario

**Fable — B2C Mental Wellness**

Fable is a mental wellness app for adults aged 25–45. Its core experience successfully helps users through periods of acute stress and anxiety, but many highly engaged users stop using the product once that immediate need is resolved. The strategic challenge is to define what Fable becomes after the acute phase and create a durable engagement model without losing the simplicity and trust that made the product successful.

---

## Playing to Win

### Winning aspiration

Help adults who have moved beyond acute anxiety continue to feel in control of their everyday mental wellbeing through a simple, trusted Fable experience they choose to return to.

### Where to play

Focus on existing Fable users aged 25–45 transitioning beyond their initial 90–120-day acute-use phase, within Fable's existing mobile experience and freemium/premium model. Prioritise ongoing meditation, sleep, breathing and proactive wellbeing use cases rather than clinical treatment, B2B expansion or new-market expansion.

### How to win

Combine Fable's trusted, judgement-free experience with its understanding of each user's engagement history to deliver personalised, low-friction post-acute check-ins, recommendations and wellbeing routines that remain useful after the original anxiety need has subsided.

### Capabilities required

Build strong post-acute lifecycle detection, behavioural segmentation, personalised recommendations, AI-assisted check-ins, contextual re-engagement and experimentation capabilities while retaining Fable's meditation and sleep content foundation and clinical-advisory safety guardrails.

### Management systems

Track post-acute retention, weekly active usage and premium renewal by cohort; review experiment and engagement performance monthly; monitor personalisation and safety signals; and conduct a quarterly strategy review to decide whether the post-acute proposition is creating durable user value.

---

## One Hard No

**We will not build a social/community layer as the primary growth strategy this quarter.**

Although community could create additional engagement, introducing a social model would materially change Fable's private and simple user experience before we have proven that personalised post-acute wellbeing can create durable retention for the users we already serve.

---

## OKR Cascade

### Objective

**Make Fable a trusted ongoing wellbeing companion that users continue to value after their acute mental-wellness need has passed.**

- **KR1:** 180-day retention among users entering the post-acute phase from the measured pre-initiative cohort baseline to **30% above baseline** by the end of Q4.
- **KR2:** Weekly active usage among post-acute users from the measured pre-initiative cohort baseline to **25% above baseline** by the end of Q4.
- **KR3:** Premium renewal among users entering the post-acute phase from the measured pre-initiative cohort baseline to **20% above baseline** by the end of Q4.

> **Measurement note:** The Fable case provides the strategic retention problem but does not provide numeric baselines for 180-day retention, weekly active usage or premium renewal. The baseline values should be populated from the pre-initiative cohort before execution; the improvement percentages above are project targets.

---

## AI Pressure-Test Summary

### Biggest assumption that could be wrong

Users whose acute anxiety has improved still want an ongoing relationship with a mental-wellness product, and personalised post-acute value can overcome their natural tendency to stop using Fable once they feel better.

**Response:** Add an explicit validation gate for post-acute demand before scaling the investment.

### Board question I cannot yet answer

What evidence proves that post-acute users want an ongoing maintenance relationship with Fable rather than simply feeling that they no longer need a wellness app?

**Response:** Validate this through cohort research and controlled post-acute engagement experiments before committing further resources.

### KRs that are outputs in disguise

None. All three KRs measure behavioural or commercial outcomes — retention, weekly active usage and premium renewal — rather than feature launches or delivery milestones.

**Response:** Defend the outcome-based KRs.

### The no I should reconsider

A social/community layer could create accountability and recurring reasons to return, potentially addressing retention differently from individual personalisation.

**Response:** Keep it outside the current strategy, but reconsider it if personalised post-acute engagement fails to produce durable retention.

### Strategy or wish list?

**Strategy.** It makes explicit choices about who Fable serves after the acute phase, where it will compete, how it intends to win, which capabilities it needs, and what valuable alternative it will deliberately not pursue. Its main unresolved risk is whether sustained post-acute demand actually exists.

---

## Final Strategy Verdict

Proceed with the post-acute retention strategy, but make validation of sustained post-acute demand the first evidence gate before scaling product and engineering investment.
