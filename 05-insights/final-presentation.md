# Final Project Presentation — Fable Growth

> Module 6 · Final submission
>
> A five-slide executive story that connects strategy, prioritisation, team operating model, financial judgment and leadership reflection.

## Presentation thesis

**Fable's growth wall is a retention problem created by product success: users resolve their acute anxiety need within roughly 90–120 days and then no longer have a reason to return. The strategy is to earn an ongoing role in everyday wellbeing without becoming clinical or sacrificing the simplicity and trust that made Fable work.**

---

## Slide 1 — Product Strategy: Earn the post-acute relationship

### Playing to Win

- **Winning aspiration:** Help adults move from acute support to an ongoing, trusted everyday wellbeing relationship with Fable.
- **Where to play:** Existing users aged 25–45 transitioning beyond the acute-use phase, within the current mobile freemium/premium model.
- **How to win:** Personalised, low-friction post-acute check-ins, recommendations and wellbeing routines built on Fable's trusted non-clinical experience.
- **Capabilities required:** Lifecycle detection, behavioural segmentation, AI-assisted check-ins, personalisation, contextual re-engagement, experimentation and safety guardrails.
- **Management systems:** Cohort retention, weekly active usage and premium renewal reviews; monthly experiments; quarterly strategy review.

### Hard No

**Do not make social/community the primary growth strategy until personalised post-acute value has been tested.**

### OKR cascade

**Objective:** Make Fable a trusted ongoing wellbeing companion after the acute need has passed.

- **KR1:** 30% relative improvement in 180-day post-acute retention by end of Q4.
- **KR2:** 25% relative improvement in weekly active usage among post-acute users by end of Q4.
- **KR3:** 20% relative improvement in premium renewal among post-acute users by end of Q4.

### 45-second talk track

Fable's problem is counterintuitive: the product works, users feel better, and then they leave. I therefore chose not to solve this with more acquisition or a broad feature expansion. The bet is to make Fable useful after the acute phase through personalised, lightweight everyday wellbeing. The Hard No is important: community may be attractive, but it changes the product before we have proven the core post-acute demand hypothesis. The three KRs measure behaviour and commercial value, not feature delivery.

---

## Slide 2 — Outcome Roadmap: Put the retention Rocks first

### Now — 0 to 3 months

1. **Personalised post-acute engagement** → create a relevant reason to return after the acute need subsides.
2. **Reliable contextual re-engagement** → increase meaningful weekly usage at the right time.
3. **Everyday wellbeing value** → extend meditation, sleep, breathing and short-form routines beyond acute moments.

### Next — 3 to 6 months

- Reduce core product friction and improve load performance.
- Reframe onboarding around an ongoing wellbeing relationship.
- Test lightweight habit mechanics only where they reinforce demonstrated value.
- Close accessibility gaps across the core experience.

### Later — 6 to 12 months

- Extend proven routines to web/wearable surfaces.
- Explore localisation after the retention model works in current markets.
- Reassess social/community as a separate retention hypothesis.
- Evaluate B2B expansion only after the core B2C model is proven.

### Trade-off memo

**Sequence first:** work that directly tests post-acute value and retention.  
**Push out:** performance, onboarding optimisation, habit mechanics and expansion until the value proposition is validated.  
**Cut from the current horizon:** therapist matching/clinical expansion and community as the primary growth strategy.

### 45-second talk track

I treated the roadmap as a sequence of bets, not a list of features. The Now horizon contains only three outcomes tied directly to the Module 1 KRs. Everything else is deliberately sequenced behind the most important learning: whether users actually want an ongoing relationship with Fable once they feel better. This prevents attractive expansion requests from consuming capacity before the retention model is proven.

## Slide 3 — Team Charter: Outcome ownership beats shared-feature ownership

### What We Own

- **PM1:** onboarding and early activation.
- **PM2:** retention and the post-acute user experience.
- **PM3:** AI-powered check-in capability.
- **Core team:** roadmap, product experiments, personalised post-acute experience and product-level measurement.
- **Out of scope:** clinical repositioning, company-wide AI infrastructure, acquisition/media strategy, B2B expansion in the current horizon.

### How We Decide

- The **PM who owns the affected customer outcome decides within that surface**.
- Shared technology does **not** transfer ownership of the customer outcome.
- Cross-surface conflicts get **one synchronous discussion** and then escalate to the **Product Lead within 24 hours** if unresolved.
- Safety/clinical concerns require Clinical Advisory review before release.
- Reopening a Hard No requires **new evidence**, not stakeholder pressure alone.

### Example: AI check-in tension

PM3 owns the AI capability; PM2 owns post-acute retention; Growth owns CRM channel operations. If an AI campaign changes the post-acute experience, PM2/PM3/Growth make a joint call; unresolved conflict escalates within 24 hours.

### 45-second talk track

The main operating tension was the AI check-in layer because it crosses onboarding, retention and Growth. The key decision was to separate capability ownership from outcome ownership. PM3 can own the AI layer without owning every experience that uses it. This makes decision rights explicit enough for a new PM to act without waiting for consensus and gives the team a fast escalation path when boundaries collide.

## Slide 4 — Financial Model: Fund the evidence, not the headline

### Course case economics — Fable Team Tier

- **$12 / seat / month**
- **6 seats** per converted team
- **4%** individual-to-team conversion assumption
- **3,000** weekly-active individuals
- **120** projected team conversions / quarter
- **$103,680** projected net new ARR in Q1
- **15%** annual team churn
- **$38** individual-user CAC

### Stress-test

- Conversion-adjusted CAC per converted team: **~$950** ($38 ÷ 4%).
- Simplified revenue LTV per team: **~$5,760** ($864 ÷ 15% churn).
- Simplified LTV:CAC: **~6.1:1**.
- Implied payback using the supplied inputs: **~13.2 months**, not the case headline of 4.7 months.
- If churn rises to 25%, LTV:CAC falls to **~3.6:1**.
- If CAC rises 20%, payback rises to **~15.8 months**.

### Funding decision

**Fund with conditions:** validate before scale; do not displace the current post-acute retention Rocks.

### Kill criterion

**If individual-to-team conversion does not reach at least 3% by the end of Q2, stop scaling the Team Tier, commit no additional Q3 headcount, and reallocate the planned capacity to the core post-acute retention roadmap.**

### 45-second talk track

The finance exercise reinforced that a product leader should not accept a model just because the headline looks attractive. The case mixes individual CAC with team-account revenue, so I normalised the units and found that the stated 4.7-month payback does not reconcile with the supplied assumptions. I would fund the learning, not the scale, and I replaced “reassess” with a kill criterion that has a metric, threshold, deadline and financial consequence.

## Slide 5 — Individual Insights: What changed in my product-leadership thinking

### Friction

Keeping every decision aligned to one diagnosis while resisting attractive but off-strategy opportunities — and avoiding false precision where the case did not provide numeric baselines.

### Key learnings

- Strategy is visible in what does **not** get funded.
- Roadmaps should express **outcomes and hypotheses**, not feature calendars.
- Decision rights prevent shared surfaces from becoming shared ambiguity.
- Financial fluency means challenging assumptions and units, not just reading outputs.
- AI creates leverage when it **pressure-tests human judgment** rather than replacing it.

### Aha!

**Strategy, roadmap, team design and finance are the same decision viewed at different levels.** The strategy names the bet; the roadmap allocates resources; the charter assigns decision rights; the business case prices the bet; and the kill criterion defines when evidence wins over conviction.

### 45-second talk track

My biggest shift was seeing product leadership as one connected system rather than separate skills. A strategy is not complete when the document is written; it becomes operational only when the roadmap, team decision rights, financial logic and stop conditions all reinforce the same choice. AI was most useful as a pressure-testing partner that exposed assumptions and contradictions while leaving the final judgment with the product leader.
