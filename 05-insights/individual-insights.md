# Individual Insights: Strategy Development Process

> Module 6 · Leverage AI Tools for Leadership Execution
>
> Personal reflection on building the Fable Growth strategy end to end.

## Friction points

_What was the hardest part of building this strategy, and how did you work through it?_

> The hardest part was keeping every downstream decision anchored to one diagnosis: **Fable does not have an acquisition problem; it has a post-acute retention problem.** The backlog contained several attractive alternatives — community, therapist matching, more content, localisation, B2B expansion and new surfaces — and many could be justified in isolation. The challenge was deciding which opportunities actually advanced the strategy and which ones created strategic drift.
>
> I worked through this by using one source of truth across the project: the Playing to Win cascade and its OKRs. Every roadmap item had to trace to a measurable outcome, every cross-team decision had to protect the same outcome ownership, and every financial bet needed an explicit stop condition. I also used AI as a devil's advocate rather than as a decision-maker: it helped expose assumptions, challenge the Hard No and identify where the plan relied on evidence that did not yet exist.
>
> A second friction point was **false precision**. The Fable case does not provide numeric baselines for post-acute retention, weekly usage or premium renewal, so I avoided inventing historical values. I kept the OKRs as relative improvement targets and made baseline instrumentation a prerequisite for operationalising them.
>
> The financial work created a third friction point: the headline payback period in the supplied case did not reconcile with the stated CAC, conversion rate and team revenue. Rather than accept the model at face value, I traced the units end to end and surfaced the inconsistency as a funding condition.

## Key learnings

_A few surprising discoveries or insights gained from the course overall._

> **1. Strategy is visible in what does not get funded.** A Playing to Win cascade only becomes real when it produces a Hard No and moves resources toward a small number of bets.
>
> **2. A roadmap is a sequence of hypotheses, not a feature calendar.** Rewriting the Now items as “We bet…” statements made the connection between product work and customer outcomes much clearer.
>
> **3. Decision rights are a product-leadership tool.** The AI check-in layer touches onboarding, retention and Growth, but shared technology does not mean shared ownership. Naming who owns the customer outcome, who owns the enabling capability and how conflicts escalate removes ambiguity before it becomes politics.
>
> **4. Financial fluency is primarily about asking the right questions.** The most important work was identifying the load-bearing assumption, checking whether CAC and LTV were expressed at the same unit, stress-testing churn and payback, and defining a kill criterion that someone outside the team could enforce.
>
> **5. AI is most valuable when it strengthens judgment rather than replaces it.** The strongest uses in this project were pressure-testing assumptions, finding contradictions, improving clarity and turning leadership standards into repeatable operating rules.

## "Aha!" moment

_The main "aha" moment during the project process._

> My biggest aha was that **strategy, execution, team design and finance are not separate product-leadership disciplines — they are the same decision viewed at different levels.** The strategy defines the bet; the roadmap decides when it earns resources; the team charter defines who can make the call; the financial model defines what the bet is worth; and the kill criterion defines when conviction must give way to evidence. A strategy is only truly operational when all five tell the same story.
