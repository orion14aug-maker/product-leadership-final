# Team Charter: Fable Growth

> Module 3 · Lead and Develop High-Performing Teams, ★ Deliverable 3
>
> Define how the Fable Growth team operates. This charter covers the two required components: **What We Own** and **How We Decide**.

## Context

Fable's current strategic priority is to build a durable engagement model for users after their acute mental-wellness need has passed. The team must improve post-acute retention and engagement without turning Fable into a clinical mental-health platform or losing the simplicity and trust of the existing experience.

The core product team has 14 people and includes three PM ownership areas:

- **PM 1 — Onboarding & Early Activation**
- **PM 2 — Retention & Post-Acute Experience**
- **PM 3 — AI-Powered Check-In Layer**

The Engineering Lead and Design Lead work with the team but report through their functional organizations. The Growth team sits outside the core product organization and has a stake in several surfaces, especially the AI-powered check-in layer.

The operating tension this charter resolves is that the AI check-in layer can affect onboarding, retention, and Growth-owned objectives at the same time. This charter makes ownership and escalation explicit so shared surfaces do not become shared ambiguity.

## 1. What We Own

_The team's mandate: the outcomes and surfaces this team is accountable for end-to-end, and the explicit edges where ownership stops._

| Area | We own it | We influence it (don't own) |
|---|---|---|
| **Post-acute retention & engagement** | End-to-end product strategy and experience for users transitioning beyond the initial acute-use phase, including the post-acute journey, recurring wellbeing routines, engagement experiments, and measurement against retention and weekly-usage outcomes. **Primary accountable PM: PM 2.** | Brand marketing, paid acquisition, pricing policy, and broader company growth targets. We provide evidence on retention and renewal implications but do not unilaterally set those strategies. |
| **Onboarding & early activation** | The in-product journey from first open through early activation, including how Fable sets expectations for an ongoing wellbeing relationship. **Primary accountable PM: PM 1.** | Acquisition-channel strategy and campaign messaging owned outside the core product team. Changes that alter acquisition promises require a joint decision with Growth. |
| **AI-powered check-in layer** | Product requirements, user experience, personalisation logic, product-level quality standards, experimentation, and safe use of the AI check-in capability. **Primary accountable PM: PM 3.** | Enterprise-wide AI infrastructure, foundational model/vendor selection, legal policy, and clinical governance decisions that sit with relevant specialist functions. |
| **Core wellbeing experience** | How meditation, sleep, breathing, short-form wellbeing content, and personalised recommendations are surfaced within the core product to support the post-acute strategy. | Editorial production standards and clinical-advisory policy. Product influences priorities, but specialist Content and Clinical Advisory owners retain their functional authority. |
| **Re-engagement inside the product strategy** | Product requirements for when, why, and to whom Fable should re-engage based on lifecycle stage and user value. | CRM channel operations and company-wide notification policy. Execution that changes global CRM rules requires agreement with Growth/CRM. |
| **Experimentation & product measurement** | Hypothesis definition, experiment design for product bets, success metrics, cohort definitions, and recommendations based on results. | Company-wide analytics infrastructure and finance reporting. The team specifies what must be measured but does not own the underlying enterprise data platform. |
| **Roadmap and scope for the Fable Growth initiative** | Sequencing and scope of the approved Fable Growth product roadmap, including the Now/Next/Later bets and trade-offs required to protect the post-acute strategy. | Company portfolio allocation outside the initiative and executive-level funding decisions. Material changes to agreed strategic outcomes require escalation. |

> **Our mission in one line:** Build a simple, trusted post-acute Fable experience that earns an ongoing place in users' everyday wellbeing and measurably improves retention, meaningful weekly usage, and premium renewal.

### Explicitly out of scope

The Fable Growth team does **not** own:

- repositioning Fable as a clinical mental-health or therapy platform;
- therapist matching or structured clinical-treatment programmes;
- B2B product expansion such as **Fable for Teams** within the current strategy horizon;
- geographic expansion and localisation as a substitute for solving the retention problem;
- company-wide AI infrastructure, procurement, legal policy, or clinical-governance policy;
- company-wide acquisition strategy, media spend, or CRM operations;
- a social/community layer as the primary growth strategy in the current horizon.

### Cross-boundary decisions that require a joint call

A PM must **not act unilaterally** when a decision crosses one of the following boundaries:

1. **AI check-in × onboarding:** Any change to the AI check-in layer that alters the onboarding or early-activation experience requires **PM 1 + PM 3** agreement.
2. **AI check-in × post-acute retention:** Any change to AI check-ins that changes post-acute journeys, frequency, recommendations, or retention experiments requires **PM 2 + PM 3** agreement.
3. **AI check-in × Growth/CRM:** Any use of the AI layer in externally operated lifecycle messaging, campaign journeys, or CRM rules requires **PM 3 + the Growth/CRM lead** agreement, with PM 2 consulted when post-acute users are affected.
4. **Clinical or safety boundary:** Any product change that could reasonably alter Fable's non-clinical positioning, safety handling, or mental-health risk profile requires **Clinical Advisory + Product Lead** review before commitment.
5. **Core strategic trade-off:** Any proposal that displaces a current-quarter Rock, changes a Module 1 OKR, or introduces a previously rejected Hard No requires a decision from the **Product Lead** rather than a local PM compromise.

## 2. How We Decide

_The team's decision-making operating model: the decisions we make, who makes each call, who must be consulted, and how disagreements resolve._

| Decision type | Who decides | Who's consulted | How we break a tie |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scope within onboarding & early activation** | **PM 1** | Design Lead, Engineering Lead; PM 3 if AI check-ins are affected; Growth if acquisition promises or CRM journeys change | If the decision crosses another owned surface and the two PMs cannot agree, escalate to the **Product Lead within 24 hours** with both options, expected OKR impact, and trade-off. |
| **Scope within post-acute retention experience** | **PM 2** | Design Lead, Engineering Lead, Data/Analytics; PM 3 for AI dependencies; Clinical Advisory when wellbeing/safety implications exist | PM 2 decides within the owned surface. Cross-surface disputes escalate to the **Product Lead within 24 hours**. |
| **AI check-in product capability** | **PM 3** | PM 1 and/or PM 2 depending on affected journey; Engineering Lead, Design Lead, Data/Analytics, Clinical Advisory | PM 3 decides technical/product scope inside the AI layer, but cannot override the PM who owns the affected user journey. Unresolved conflicts go to the **Product Lead within 24 hours**. |
| **Post-acute experiment design & success criteria** | **PM 2** | PM 3 for AI-enabled experiments, Data/Analytics, Design, Engineering, Clinical Advisory where relevant | The accountable PM decides after consultation. If teams disagree about the primary metric or whether the experiment risks an existing OKR, the **Product Lead** makes the call. |
| **Notification or re-engagement changes** | **PM 2** for product intent; **Growth/CRM lead** for channel operations | PM 3 for AI-generated content, Engineering, Data/Analytics | No unilateral override. If product value and Growth objectives conflict, the **Product Lead and Growth Lead hold a synchronous decision meeting within 24 hours**. |
| **Current-quarter roadmap trade-offs** | **Product Lead** | PM 1, PM 2, PM 3, Engineering Lead, Design Lead; Growth/Clinical Advisory when affected | Product Lead makes the final call based on impact on the agreed OKRs, cost of delay, effort, and opportunity cost. |
| **Changes to a strategic Hard No** | **Product Lead** | Relevant PM, Growth Lead, Engineering Lead, Design Lead, and specialist owner where applicable | A Hard No remains closed unless new evidence materially changes the strategic case. The Product Lead decides after a documented review; stakeholder pressure alone is not sufficient evidence. |
| **Clinical/safety boundary** | **Clinical Advisory owns the safety/clinical judgment; Product Lead owns the product response** | Relevant PM, Legal if required, Engineering/Design | If Clinical Advisory identifies an unacceptable safety or regulatory risk, the change does not ship until the concern is resolved. |
| **Engineering feasibility and architecture** | **Engineering Lead** | Owning PM, relevant engineers, PM 3 when AI architecture is involved | Engineering Lead has final say on technical feasibility and architecture; Product Lead decides whether the resulting cost/timeline still justifies the product bet. |
| **Design-system and interaction-quality calls** | **Design Lead** | Owning PM, relevant designer, Engineering Lead | Design Lead decides interaction/design standards; Product Lead resolves only when the decision creates a material strategic or roadmap trade-off. |

> **Our default:** The PM who owns the affected customer outcome decides within that surface; we escalate to the **Product Lead within 24 hours** when a decision crosses ownership boundaries, changes a committed Rock or OKR, or cannot be resolved by the accountable PMs in one synchronous discussion.

### Decision rules

1. **Outcome ownership beats feature ownership.** The PM accountable for the customer outcome retains decision rights even when another team's capability powers the experience.
2. **Shared technology does not mean shared product ownership.** PM 3 owns the AI check-in capability; PM 1 and PM 2 retain ownership of the onboarding and post-acute outcomes where that capability appears.
3. **Consultation is required; consensus is not.** Decision owners must hear relevant expertise, but a decision does not remain open simply because every stakeholder does not agree.
4. **Escalate quickly, not repeatedly.** Cross-team disagreements get one direct synchronous discussion. If unresolved, they go to the Product Lead within 24 hours rather than continuing through Slack or recurring meetings.
5. **Use the strategy as the tie-breaker.** When two options are both viable, prefer the one that most directly improves post-acute retention, meaningful weekly usage, or premium renewal without violating Fable's non-clinical positioning and trust principles.
6. **Reopening a decision requires new evidence.** Seniority, urgency, or repeated requests are not by themselves reasons to reopen a prioritization call.
7. **Safety overrides velocity.** A credible clinical, legal, privacy, or user-safety concern pauses release until the relevant specialist owner and Product Lead agree on a safe path forward.

## Operating example: resolving the AI check-in tension

**Situation:** Growth wants PM 3 to use the AI check-in layer for a new re-engagement campaign, while PM 2 believes the requested frequency will undermine the post-acute experience.

**How this charter resolves it:**

1. **PM 3 owns the AI capability**, including what the layer can technically and safely generate.
2. **PM 2 owns the post-acute user outcome**, including the engagement experience and retention experiment.
3. **Growth/CRM owns channel operations**, but cannot unilaterally change the product's post-acute engagement logic.
4. PM 2, PM 3, and the Growth/CRM lead meet synchronously and assess the proposal against the current retention and weekly-usage outcomes.
5. If they cannot resolve the trade-off in that discussion, the issue goes to the **Product Lead within 24 hours**.
6. The Product Lead makes the final call based on the agreed strategy, OKR impact, user trust, safety, and opportunity cost.

This prevents the AI layer from becoming a shared surface where decision rights are unclear while still allowing Growth to influence how the capability creates value.

## Strategic traceability

This charter exists to execute the Fable strategy and roadmap, not as a generic team operating agreement.

- **Strategy:** Make Fable a trusted ongoing wellbeing companion after the acute phase.
- **Primary customer outcome:** Durable post-acute retention.
- **Now bets:** Personalised post-acute engagement, reliable contextual re-engagement, and recurring everyday wellbeing value.
- **Protected trade-off:** Do not let social/community, clinical expansion, B2B expansion, or geographic expansion displace the core post-acute retention bet without new evidence and an explicit Product Lead decision.
